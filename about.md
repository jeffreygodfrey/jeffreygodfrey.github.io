I know that what people are expecting me to say is that I'm something, but honestly I've never really felt like I was much of anything that people would consider important. I'm usually identified by what I am to other people. Sure, I am something to other people, for instance in no particular order of importance I’m someone’s son, sibling, husband, father, co-worker, etc. but none of those things are what I am to me. I’m good at a lot of things, and most of those things are related to some kind of technology. You see, I have this knack for technology, always have.

Growing up I would take things apart and put them back together. I always wanted to know what was inside things and how those things worked. I’ll find a new thing and before I realize it, I'm entranced about how the thing works and this feeling doesn’t end until I figure out the thing. This is a constant motivator and sometimes a detriment, this entanglement with all things electronic and mechanical.

Years ago, my uncle had just built a computer to replace his old one, and was giving the old computer to me. His old computer was a [Coleco Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleco_Adam). This wasn't what you'd consider a computer by today's standards. It ran programs off of cassette tapes, and stored data to cassette tapes. This is about the time I was introduced to the programming language [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC). We were given everything that went with the computer, including the cassette tapes that contained programs and data. On these tapes I found some strange files that were somewhat easy to follow. I learned that these were programs. Programs that instructed the computer to do tasks. Tasks that would normally be performed by you, but instead these programs issued a set of commands that the computer understood so you wouldn’t have to type the commands again each time. I didn't know it at the time, but I was hooked. I toyed around with writing programs, very simple programs. It was interesting.

Today I play video games on my PC and on a console. It’s core to my personality. Of course I do other things, like work and hang out with my family. My kids are into playing video games as well, so we often spend time playing a game together. My wife isn’t all that into playing video games, but she does like to play games and we’ll often play Yahtzee, Sorry, or something else from our collection of board games. I truly enjoy my time at home with my family.

I mentioned before, but I like to tinker. It wasn't until I started working in the IT field that I had some reason and purpose to have scripts that I'd use often. The reason is because I'm lazy and I don't like typing or moving the mouse over and over to do the same menial task. I found vbscript and liked using it for a lot of stuff at first, then moved on to powershell. I learned about using vba in excel to create buttons that would filter and sort raw data. A new role I took on opened up opportunities to simplify and semi-automate seemingly menial tasks. I also build simple tools for myself at work.

That kind of got me to this point where I finally strapped myself in and started trying to learn modern web design. I’m running this website using Github Pages so there is no access to server side scripting, but i’ve managed to find some ways to make things work without it. Nearly everything on this website is dynamically generated, the nav, the article cards you saw on the way to this page, etc. I use dynamic generation loosely here because what it really does is read from a json file and frontmatter on markdown files.
